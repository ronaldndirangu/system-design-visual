## The failure: a disk that fills in a month

A small team ships a URL shortener on one server with a 100 GB disk. Nobody does the math. A few months later the disk is
full, writes start failing, and every new short link returns an error.

<Predict

  id="thinking/requirements-and-estimates/predict-1"

  question="The growing startup scale adds 1 million links a day at about 500 bytes each. How much storage do 5 years of links need?"
  options={['About 1 GB', 'About 100 GB', 'About 1 TB', 'About 100 TB']}

>

1,000,000 links × 365 days × 5 years ≈ 1.8 billion links. At 500 bytes each, that is about **900 GB**, close to 1 TB.
A 100 GB disk lasts about 200 days at this rate, and much less if traffic grows. Ten minutes of arithmetic would have caught it.

</Predict>

## Requirements come first

Before drawing a single box, write down two lists.

- **Functional requirements** say what the system does: create a short link, and redirect anyone who opens it.
- **Non-functional requirements** say how well it does it: redirects in under 100 ms, the service stays up 99.9% of the
  time, and a link never changes once created.

Then list what you will **not** build yet, such as analytics or custom aliases. Saying no out loud keeps the design small.

## Estimates in five lines

Aim for the right **order of magnitude** and round freely: a day has about 100,000 seconds
(86,400 exactly).

```text
writes per second = new links per day ÷ 86,400
reads per second  = writes per second × clicks per link
peak per second   = (writes + reads) × peak factor
storage           = links per day × 365 × years × bytes per link
servers           = peak ÷ (one server's capacity × 0.7)
```

The 0.7 keeps each server at most 70% busy, so a traffic spike or one lost server does not tip the rest over.

A few things to notice:

- **Reads dwarf writes.** At 100 clicks per link, reads are 99% of all traffic. That is why the next lessons focus on
  making reads cheap.
- **Storage grows forever** unless links expire. Retention is a product decision with a big infrastructure bill.
- **The short code barely grows.** Each extra base62 character multiplies capacity by 62, so 7 characters cover trillions
  of links.

## Trade-offs

<TradeoffTable title="Every requirement has a price">

| Requirement                    | What it buys                       | What it costs                                  |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Redirects under 100 ms         | Links feel instant                 | Caches and servers close to users              |
| 99.99% uptime instead of 99.9% | 8 hours less downtime a year       | Redundant servers, databases, and failover     |
| Keep links forever             | No broken links in old posts       | Storage that grows without limit               |
| Custom aliases                 | Memorable links such as sho.rt/sale | A uniqueness check on every write              |
| Click analytics                | Insight for link owners            | A write on every click, so writes match reads  |

</TradeoffTable>

<Challenge>

Estimate a **paste sharing site**. People save 100,000 text snippets a day, each about 10 KB. Every paste is read 10 times
on average, and pastes stay for 2 years. Work out writes per second, reads per second, and total storage.

<div slot="answer">

- Writes: 100,000 ÷ 86,400 ≈ **1.2 per second**.
- Reads: 1.2 × 10 ≈ **12 per second**. One small server handles this easily.
- Storage: 100,000 × 365 × 2 × 10 KB ≈ **730 GB**. Storage drives this design far more than traffic does, so cheap
  object storage beats a bigger database server.

</div>
