## The failure: a confident answer with no source

Two stores share the support assistant: Harbor Bikes and Pine Street Books. A Harbor customer asks, "Can I return a
sale item?" The model has never seen Harbor's policies, so it writes something plausible:

> **Fixture answer:** Yes! Sale items can be returned within 60 days for a full refund.

It reads like a policy, and none of it is true. Harbor's policy says final sale items cannot be returned. With no
documents to work from, the model filled the gap with a fluent guess.

So the team adds retrieval: find the relevant policy text and put it in the prompt. Three new failures show up at once.

- **Chunking splits the rule.** The policy is cut every 16 words. The chunk that ranks first ends with "Items can be
  returned within 30 days of", and "except final sale items" sits in the next chunk. The assistant answers "Yes" from a
  true sentence.
- **Keyword search misses a paraphrase.** "Can I send back cycling shoes I never wore?" shares no word with the return
  policy. Keyword search returns a note about back ordered parts instead, because it contains "send" and "back".
- **Vector search misses an exact code.** "Can I use code TRAIL25?" means little beyond "discount", so vector search
  ranks the promo chunk fourth and answers from the sale policy.

And with one index for both stores, nothing stops Pine Street's return rule from answering a Harbor customer.

## The idea: two searches, one ranking

Keyword search and vector search fail on different questions, so a hybrid system runs both and merges the results. The
merge is the hard part: a keyword score of 4.2 and a cosine similarity of 0.93 sit on different scales, and adding them
lets one search drown out the other.

**Reciprocal rank fusion** (RRF) sidesteps the scales by using only ranks. A chunk at rank r in a list earns
1/(k + r), with ranks starting at 1, and its fused score is the sum over both lists. A chunk missing from a list gets
0 from that list. The usual k is 60, the value used in the paper that introduced RRF. A large k keeps one first
place from dominating, and the exact value matters much less than the decision to fuse by rank.

For the sale item question at Harbor, both searches rank the main return rule first, so it scores 1/61 + 1/61, about
0.0328. The gift card chunk ranks second for keywords (it mentions sale items) and is missing from the vector list, so
it scores only 1/62, about 0.0161. Agreement between the lists beats one strong placement.

## Chunking: keep the rule with its exception

Retrieval can only send what a chunk holds. Harbor's return policy is 45 words. Split it every 16 words with no
overlap, and the first chunk ends at "within 30 days of" while the exception, "except final sale items", lands in the
next one. The first chunk matches the question best, so the model gets the rule without the exception and answers
"Yes" with confidence.

<Predict
  client:visible
  question="Which setting sends the model a chunk that holds both the 30-day rule and the sale exception?"
  options={['8 words, no overlap', '12 words, no overlap', '16 words, overlap 8']}
  answer={2}
>

**16 words, overlap 8.** Each chunk starts 8 words after the previous one, so one chunk spans the old boundary and holds
both clauses. Chunks of 8 or 12 words are shorter than the 13 words from the rule to the end of its exception, so no
overlap can save them. Larger chunks also work on this policy: every setting from 24 words up keeps the clauses
together. The policy and the matching rule are fixtures, and the results are illustrative.

</Predict>

<ChunkingWidget client:visible />

What to look for:

- **16 words, no overlap is wrong.** The sent chunk has the 30-day rule and no exception, so the fixture answer says
  yes. A true sentence, cut short, produces a wrong answer.
- **Overlap costs storage.** 16 words with overlap 8 makes 5 chunks where no overlap makes 3. Every repeated word is
  indexed and embedded twice.
- **Big chunks blur topics.** 24 words makes 2 chunks and keeps the clauses together. On a longer document, chunks this
  size mix several topics, which dilutes each embedding and fills the prompt with words the question did not need.

In practice, split on the document's structure first: headings, paragraphs, and sentences. A rule and its exception
usually share a sentence, so a splitter that respects sentences keeps them together without guessing a size. Fixed
windows with overlap are the fallback for text without structure. Whatever you choose, test it on the eval questions:
the chunking only matters through the answers it produces.

## Tenant scoping: filter before you rank

Harbor and Pine share one assistant and one index. Every chunk carries its store and who may read it. A Harbor customer
must never see Pine's policies, and no customer may see staff-only notes. There are three places to enforce that.

- **No filter.** Search every chunk. For the sale item question, Pine's return rule ranks second and is cited for a
  Harbor customer. Ask Harbor "Do you match a lower price from another shop?" and the fixture answer comes
  from Pine's price match policy, a promise Harbor never made.
- **Post-filter.** Search every chunk, take the top 3, then drop the ones this customer may not read. Nothing leaks, but
  the dropped chunks leave empty slots. For the price match question both Pine chunks drop and one chunk remains. For
  the paraphrase only the back order note remains, and the assistant abstains although Harbor's return rule answers it.
- **Pre-filter.** Limit both searches to chunks this customer may read, then rank. All three slots go to readable
  chunks. The paraphrase finds the return rule, and the price match question abstains correctly because Harbor has no
  such policy.

Enforce the scope in the search itself. An instruction in the prompt such as "use only Harbor documents" is a request
the model can ignore, and the other store's text has already reached it. Retrieved text is also an attack surface:
indirect prompt injection arrives through external content such as files and retrieved documents, so treat every
chunk as data, never as instructions. [Agents and tools](../agents/) builds that boundary. When one store grows large, giving it its own index is
[sharding](../../scaling/sharding/) by tenant, and the filter becomes a routing decision.

### Caches need the same scope

A cache in front of retrieval or answers must respect the same boundaries. Key the answer cache on the question text
alone, and Pine's cached answer to "Can I return a sale item?" can reach a Harbor customer. An exact cache key must
include every input that changes the answer: the store, the user's permission scope, the model, the prompt version, and
the sampling settings. The hybrid search widget shows the key it would use, with the store and scope first.

A semantic cache, which reuses an answer for a question that looks similar, adds a second risk. Two questions can look
alike and need different answers, and no fixed similarity threshold guarantees that a reused answer is right. The
general rules for keys, freshness, and invalidation are in [caching](../../scaling/caching/), and
[the AI gateway](../gateway/) shows where an answer cache sits and what its key must hold.

## Citations and abstention

Each chunk goes to the model with its id, and the answer cites the ids it relied on. Citations let a reader, a support
agent, or an automated check confirm every claim against the source. In this lesson a sent chunk counts as a citation
only when it clears the evidence threshold.

The fused RRF score cannot serve as evidence. It encodes rank only: a chunk that tops both lists scores 2/61 whether it
answers the question or not. Evidence needs its own check. The toy model uses a simplified **support** score, the larger
of the cosine similarity and the share of the question's keyword weight that the chunk contains. Real systems often use
a reranking model or a check that asks whether the chunk supports the answer. When the best sent chunk falls below the
threshold, the assistant abstains: "I could not find this in the store policy. Want me to connect you with a person?"

<RetrievalAbstainWidget client:visible />

The fixture eval has 12 questions across both stores. Four of them have no answer in the policies, so the right
response is to abstain.

- **At 0.50** the assistant answers everything: 8 correct and 4 wrong, one wrong answer for every question the policies
  cannot answer.
- **At 0.80**, the default, it gets 7 correct and 1 wrong, and abstains on 4, three of them correctly. "Can I pay in
  installments?" is still answered from the gift card chunk, which mentions paying for orders (support 0.85). "Is gift
  wrap free all year?" is abstained although Pine's gift wrap chunk answers it: free only in December.
- **At 0.85** the wrong answers reach 0. Correct answers drop to 6 and abstentions rise to 6, because the back ordered
  brake pads question now falls below the bar too.

A higher bar trades wrong answers for handoffs. A wrong refund answer costs money and trust; a handoff costs a person's
time. Pick the threshold with an eval set, as in [evaluation and budgets](../evaluation-and-budgets/), and count both
kinds of miss. All of these counts come from the fixture and are illustrative.

## Trade-offs

<TradeoffTable title="Building retrieval">

| Choice | What it buys | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small chunks | Precise matches and short prompts | Rules split from their exceptions, and more chunks to index |
| Large chunks or overlap | Related clauses stay together | Blurred embeddings, longer prompts, and repeated text to store |
| Keyword search only | Exact codes, names, and rare terms | Misses paraphrases |
| Vector search only | Paraphrases and related meaning | Misses exact tokens, and needs an embedding model and a vector index |
| Hybrid with RRF | Both kinds of match, with no score weights to tune | Two indexes to build and keep in sync, and two searches per question |
| Pre-filter by store and role | No leaks, and every slot holds a readable chunk | Every chunk needs store and permission labels, and the index must filter fast |
| Post-filter after top-k | Easy to add to an existing search | Can leave too few chunks, and the answer that existed never arrives |
| High abstain threshold | Fewer confident wrong answers | More handoffs, including some questions the policy could answer |
| Low abstain threshold | Fewer handoffs | More wrong answers built from weak matches |

</TradeoffTable>
