## The failure: two leaders fork the chat

The chat service keeps its message history on five servers. One server, the leader, takes every new message and copies
it to the others in order, the same primary-and-copies shape as [read replicas](../../scaling/read-replicas/). Every
server shows the same history, until the leader crashes.

Someone has to take over. The naive rule says: any server that stops hearing from the leader promotes itself. Two
servers notice the silence at about the same moment, and both promote themselves. One friend’s “noon?” lands on one,
and another friend’s “6 pm?” lands on the other. The chat now has two histories, and when they meet, one side must
throw away messages that its users already saw as sent. This is **split brain**.

It gets worse. A leader that is slow, or cut off by the network, looks exactly like a crashed one. It keeps running,
keeps believing it leads, and keeps accepting messages.

## The idea: terms, votes, and a majority

Raft is a consensus algorithm: a way for a group of servers to agree on one ordered log, here the chat history, while
some of them crash or get cut off. It rests on five rules.

- **Terms.** Time is split into numbered terms. Each term starts with an election and has at most one leader. Every
  message carries the term of its sender. A server that sees a higher term adopts it and becomes a follower on the
  spot, and everyone rejects messages from an older term. A stale leader learns it is stale the first time it hears
  from anyone.
- **Randomized election timeouts.** The leader sends a heartbeat every 50 ms. Each follower draws a random timeout
  between 150 and 300 ms and restarts it on every heartbeat. When it runs out, the follower becomes a **candidate**: it
  moves to the next term, votes for itself, and asks everyone else for a vote. Random timers make it likely that one
  server wakes up first and wins before the others start.
- **One vote per term, for an up-to-date log only.** Each server grants at most one vote per term, to the first
  candidate that asks. It refuses a candidate whose log is behind its own: an older term on the last entry, or the same
  term and fewer entries. A candidate needs a majority, 3 of 5.
- **Append with a consistency check.** The leader appends each chat message to its log and sends it to the followers.
  Each send names the entry just before the new ones, by position and term. A follower whose log disagrees at that
  point says no, and the leader retries one entry earlier until the logs match. The follower then deletes everything
  after the match that conflicts and copies the entries of the leader.
- **Commit on a majority.** Once a majority stores an entry from the current term of the leader, the leader commits
  it: the chat shows the message as sent, and the leader passes the new commit point to followers on its next message.
  A committed entry never changes. Older-term entries commit along with the first current-term entry after them.

Majorities do the heavy lifting. Any two groups of 3 out of 5 share at least one server, the overlap from
[Quorums](../quorums/). So two candidates cannot both win a term, and every winning candidate collects a vote from at
least one server that stores every committed entry. That server refuses a candidate with an older log, so each new
leader already holds the whole committed chat.

## Commit on a majority

In Leader crash, Node 2 wins term 1 at 0.19 s, and the first three chat messages commit at 0.44, 0.64, and 0.84 s. At
1.00 s Node 2 crashes. At 1.10 s a user sends “noon?”, and with no leader to take it, the chat app holds it.

- The timer of Node 3 runs out at 1.13 s. Node 3 asks for votes in term 2 and wins at 1.19 s.
- Node 3 appends “noon?”, and the message commits at 1.25 s, once 3 of 5 servers store it.
- The chat had no working leader for 190 ms.
- Node 2 comes back at 2.00 s, sees term 2, stays a follower, and catches up from Node 3.

Waiting for 3 copies is what makes the commit safe. Any future leader needs 3 votes, and at least one of those voters
stores “noon?” and refuses a candidate whose log lacks it. So a committed message shows up in the log of every later
leader. The unit tests of the model check this after every tick, in all three scenarios with 50 seeds each, along with
at most one leader per term.

## When the old leader is cut off

A crash is the easy case. A network partition is harder, because the old leader keeps running and still believes it
leads. In Old leader cut off, the network splits at 1.00 s: Node 2 (the term 1 leader) and Node 3 can reach only each
other, and Nodes 1, 4, and 5 can reach only each other.

1. At 1.10 s, Node 2 accepts “tacos?”. Only 2 of 5 servers store it, so it can never commit, and its sender never gets
   a confirmation.
2. On the majority side, the timer of Node 1 runs out at 1.18 s. Node 1 wins term 2 at 1.24 s, and “noon?” commits at
   1.30 s.
3. At 1.40 s, Node 2 accepts “6 pm?”, which cannot commit either.
4. The network heals at 2.00 s. At 2.02 s Node 2 sees term 2, steps down, and erases “tacos?” and “6 pm?”, because they
   conflict with the log of the new leader. Node 3 erases the same two entries 10 ms later.

Every server ends with the same chat: hi, hey!, noon?, sure, see you. Five of seven messages committed, the two sent to
the old leader were lost, and the chat had no working leader for 240 ms. No committed message was lost. Neither erased
message was ever confirmed, so a real chat client would time out and resend it to the new leader. That resend is why
each message needs an ID the server can check for duplicates, which the Idempotency lesson covers.

The minority side stayed consistent by refusing to commit: it gave up availability for writes during the partition.
That is the choice [CAP and PACELC](../cap-and-pacelc/) describes, and Raft always makes it the same way.

## What this simplified Raft leaves out

Real Raft, as used in etcd (the store behind Kubernetes), Consul, and CockroachDB, adds pieces this model skips:

- **Snapshots.** Real logs would grow forever, so servers compact old entries into a snapshot.
- **Membership changes.** Adding or removing a server safely takes a special two-step configuration change.
- **Pre-vote.** A server returning from a partition can bump the term and disrupt a healthy leader. Pre-vote asks
  first whether it could win.
- **The no-op entry.** A new leader appends an empty entry at the start of its term, so older entries commit promptly.
- **Client retries and safe reads.** Clients resend after a timeout, and a leader confirms it still leads before it
  answers a read, so a cut-off leader never serves stale chat. [Consistency models](../consistency-models/) explains
  what those reads promise.
- **Disk.** Real servers write their term, vote, and log to disk before they reply. The model keeps all three through a
  crash as if they were on disk.

## Trade-offs

<TradeoffTable title="Raft choices">

| Choice                            | What it buys                                    | What it costs                                                  |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Five servers instead of three     | Survives two failures instead of one            | Each commit waits for 3 copies instead of 2, with more traffic |
| Shorter election timeout          | Faster failover after a real crash              | Needless elections when the network or a server is only slow   |
| Wider random timeout range        | Fewer split votes                               | A slower worst case before anyone stands for election          |
| Raft for every chat message       | One agreed order, and no confirmed message lost | Every write waits for a majority round trip through one leader |
| Leader checks it still leads      | Reads never show stale chat                     | An extra heartbeat round (or a time-based lease) for each read |
| Servers spread across three zones | Survives losing a whole zone                    | Cross-zone round trips add to every commit                     |

</TradeoffTable>

<div slot="answer">

- **Any one zone.** Losing a two-server zone leaves 3 of 5, still a majority. With a three-and-two layout across two
  zones, losing the three-server zone would stop all writes, so spread the servers until no zone holds a majority.
- **Shorter timeouts trade safety margin for speed.** Failover takes roughly one election timeout plus a vote round
  trip. Timeouts under 100 ms sit close to the heartbeat interval and to cross-zone round trips, so a long garbage
  collection pause or a few lost packets start needless elections, and each one pauses writes. Keep the heartbeat
  interval far below the timeout, and the timeout far below the time between real failures. Let the chat app hold and
  resend messages during the gap, the way the model holds “noon?”.
- **Followers are missing heartbeats.** The leader may be overloaded, a server may stall in long pauses, or one link
  may drop packets. Fix the slow leader or link, raise the timeout, or turn on pre-vote so one flaky server cannot keep
  forcing new terms.

</div>

</Challenge>
