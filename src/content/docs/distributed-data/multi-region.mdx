## The failure: one region goes dark

The [chat app](../../case-studies/chat/) serves 50 million daily users from one region in Virginia. Its gateways,
servers, and [sharded](../../scaling/sharding/) message store run in three zones there, with each shard's primary in
one zone and replicas in the other two.

Zones guard against a fire or a power cut in one building. In December 2021, network congestion inside
AWS's us-east-1 region in Virginia impaired many services for hours.

## Zones, regions, and the speed of light

A **zone** is one or more datacenters with their own power, about 1 ms from the other zones in its region: close enough
for synchronous replication. A **region** is a group of zones in one metro area. Regions sit hundreds to thousands of
km apart, and light in fiber covers about 200 km per ms.

Illustrative round trips:

| From     | To        | Round trip  |
| -------- | --------- | ----------- |
| Virginia | Frankfurt | about 90 ms |
| Virginia | Singapore | about 230 ms |
| Frankfurt | Singapore | about 160 ms |

Every cross-region step in a request adds one of these. Teams go multi-region for three reasons:

- **Survive a region loss.** Another region takes over within minutes.
- **Serve far users faster.** A user in Singapore reaches a nearby region in a few ms, against 230 ms to Virginia.
- **Keep data in place.** Some data must stay in a given country or group of countries.

## Send each user to a nearby region

**GeoDNS** answers a lookup with a region near the resolver that asked. A 60 s TTL should move users within a minute, yet some resolvers and apps keep old answers far longer.

**Anycast** announces one IP address from many sites, and internet routing (BGP) carries each user to a nearby one, as
[CDNs](../../scaling/cdn/) do for their edges. When routes shift, an open connection can land at a site that never saw
it, and the socket breaks.

Chat sockets break during a failover either way. The phone reconnects, sends its cursor (the last sequence it holds),
and fetches everything after it.

<details>
<summary>Go deeper: where GeoDNS thinks you are</summary>

GeoDNS sees the resolver, which may sit far from the user: a company VPN, or a public resolver in another country. The
**EDNS Client Subnet** extension lets a resolver pass part of the user's address, so the answer fits the user. Many
privacy focused resolvers send no subnet. Apps can also time a request to each region and pick the fastest themselves.

</details>

## Where the writes go

Reads are easy: copy the data to every region and read locally. Writes decide the design.

- **One home region.** Every write goes to Virginia, and other regions hold [read replicas](../../scaling/read-replicas/).
  A user in Berlin pays about 90 ms per send, and may miss her own message on a lagging replica, as in [consistency
  models](../consistency-models/).
- **A home region per conversation.** Each conversation lives in one region near most of its members, which numbers its
  messages. The store is already sharded by conversation, so a shard map records each home.
- **Active-active.** Every region accepts writes for every conversation. Two regions can write the same thing at once,
  and last writer wins throws one away: a PA/EL choice from [CAP and PACELC](../cap-and-pacelc/).
- **Consensus across regions.** A [Raft](../raft/) group spanning three regions loses no commit in a region loss, and
  every commit waits 90 ms or more. Keep it for small, critical data, such as the map from conversation to home region.

## Failover, failback, and evacuation

Failover for Virginia's conversations runs in five steps:

1. **Detect.** Probes from other regions fail three times, 10 s apart: 30 s.
2. **Decide.** Confirm the region is down, and the probes' own path is fine.
3. **Promote.** Virginia’s primary holds a lease that a majority renews, and stops writing when it expires.
   Then Frankfurt’s replica becomes primary for those conversations.
4. **Fence.** Frankfurt takes a higher epoch in the shard map, and chat servers refuse routes with an older one, as in
   [time, leases, and fencing](../time-leases-fencing/).
5. **Reroute.** GeoDNS drops Virginia, and phones reconnect with their cursors.

Cross-region replication runs asynchronously, so lag becomes data loss. At 23,148 messages per second, with half the
conversations homed in Virginia, 0.5 s of lag strands about 5,800 acknowledged messages. That lag is the failover's
RPO, as in [backups and recovery](../../reliability/backups-and-recovery/).

Survivors also need room. With N regions sharing traffic evenly, run each at no more than (N−1)/N of its capacity: half
with two regions, two thirds with three.

<StepDiagram client:visible title="Failing over the chat from Virginia to Frankfurt" nodes={nodes} edges={edges} steps={steps} height={320} />

**Split brain.** If the link between Virginia and Frankfurt breaks while both stay up, each may promote itself. Put a
witness in a third region and promote only with 2 of 3 votes, as in Raft. The lease and epoch above stop the losing
side from writing.

**Evacuation.** A region can turn slow or flaky while its health checks pass, a **gray failure**. Drain it: move its
users elsewhere on purpose, then fix it. Drain regions on a schedule, so failover becomes routine.

<details>
<summary>Go deeper: failing back</summary>

When Virginia returns, rebuild its database as a replica of Frankfurt. Writes it took that never replicated live only
on the old primary: find them and copy back the ones you want, as with a side copy after a restore. Then move traffic
back a few percent at a time.

</details>

## Data that must stay put

Some laws and contracts require certain personal data to stay in a country or group of countries. Pin the home region
of that data to an allowed region, and keep its replicas, backups, logs, caches, and failover targets inside the same
set. A jurisdiction with one region has nowhere local to fail over, so it needs two.

## Trade-offs

<TradeoffTable title="What a region loss costs each design">

| Choice                           | What it buys                             | What it costs                               |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| One region, three zones          | Simple, fast synchronous writes          | A region loss takes everything down         |
| One home region, remote replicas | Fast local reads                         | Far writes pay a round trip                 |
| Home region per conversation     | Local sends, one order per conversation  | A shard map, and mixed conversations wait   |
| Active-active                    | Local writes everywhere                  | Conflicting writes to merge or lose         |
| Consensus across three regions   | No acknowledged write lost               | Every commit waits 90 ms or more            |
| Witness plus fencing             | Automatic failover without split brain   | A third region to run                       |

</TradeoffTable>

## Common mistakes

- Counting three zones as protection from a region loss.
- Running each region near full, so the survivors fall over during a failover.
- Promoting automatically on both sides of a partition, with no witness.
- Letting replicas, backups, or failover targets sit outside the regions the data may live in.
- Never draining a region until the day one fails.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Zones share a region's network and control systems. Surviving a region loss takes a second region.
- Every cross-region step costs a round trip: about 90 ms between Virginia and Frankfurt.
- A home region per conversation keeps one order and keeps most writes local.
- Replication lag is a failover's RPO. Promote only after the old primary's lease expires, and use a witness to avoid
  split brain.
- With N regions, run each at no more than (N−1)/N, and drain regions on a schedule to prove it.

</KeyTakeaways>

<div slot="answer">

- **Pairs per jurisdiction.** Run Virginia and Oregon for the US, and Frankfurt and Ireland for the EU. US only
  conversations live in the US pair. Any conversation with an EU member lives in the EU pair, and its US members pay

- **Failover.** Replicate asynchronously within each pair, and alert when lag passes 2 s, inside the 5 s RPO. A witness

  after that, under a higher epoch. Detection takes 30 s, leaving most of the 10
