## The failure: analytics slows down

At 8:03 p.m., the analytics ingest service starts compacting a full disk, and its calls slow from 1 ms to 3 s. Each
sho.rt worker reports the click to ingest before it sends the 302, so every redirect now waits 3 s. Within a second,
all 16 workers on every server are waiting. New clicks queue, then time out at the load balancer.

At 8:05, edges start retrying failed redirects, and app servers retry each failed ingest call. At 8:09, ingest
recovers, yet the retries keep load at several times normal and the outage continues. Meanwhile, creators whose
Shorten button spun for ten seconds press it again: 340 of them end up with two codes for one URL.

## The idea: the click never waits on the count

A redirect has one job: send the 302. Counting the click matters, and it can happen a few seconds later. Every fix in
this case keeps the core job independent of its helpers: bound each wait, cap each retry, park side work in a buffer,
and make every write safe to repeat.

## Step 1: Timeouts and a deadline

Set a timeout for the ingest call and a deadline for the whole redirect. Then count how many redirects a second the
fleet finishes if every ingest call runs into your timeout. Use
[how long a timeout should be](../../reliability/timeouts/#how-long-should-a-timeout-be) for the first number.

<details>
<summary>Show one possible answer</summary>

- **Ingest timeout**: 20 ms, about three times the healthy p99 of 6 ms.
- **Deadline**: 100 ms for the whole redirect, the same as the latency target. Each call gets the time that remains,
  and the worker skips the click report when less than 20 ms is left. See
  [one deadline for the whole trip](../../reliability/timeouts/#one-deadline-for-the-whole-trip).
- **Capacity with ingest down**: each redirect holds a worker for 2.5 + 20 = 22.5 ms. 16 ÷ 0.0225 ≈ 710 a second per
  server, about 11,000 across 16 servers. That is under a quarter of the peak.
- The timeout keeps servers alive and still fails most clicks. Step 3 removes the wait.

</details>

## Step 2: Retries with a budget

Two layers retry today. CDN edges try a failed redirect up to 3 times, and app servers try each ingest call up to 4
times. Decide where retries belong and how many each layer may send.

**About 12.** Retries multiply across layers. Each of the 3 edge tries runs up to 4 ingest calls, so one click sends
12 calls to the service that is already struggling. This is how
[retries made the outage worse](../../reliability/retries/#why-retries-made-it-worse) after ingest recovered.

<details>
<summary>Show one possible answer</summary>

- **One layer**: edges keep one retry for redirects. App servers stop retrying ingest inside a request.
- **Budget**: retries stay under 10% of fresh requests. At 50,000 a second, that allows 5,000 retries, so a full
  outage raises load by at most 10%. On a normal day about 0.1% of redirects fail, and the budget never binds. See
  [attempt limits and retry budgets](../../reliability/retries/#cap-the-total-attempt-limits-and-retry-budgets).
- **Backoff**: exponential with full jitter, so retries from thousands of readers spread out in time.
- **Only safe requests**: redirects are reads and safe to retry. Creates wait for the key in Step 4.

</details>

## Step 3: A breaker and a buffer

Take the click report out of the redirect. Size a local buffer that rides out a five minute ingest outage, and decide
when the uploader stops calling ingest and how it comes back.

<details>
<summary>Show one possible answer</summary>

- **Buffer**: the worker appends the click and sends the 302. A redirect drops to 2.5 ms, so the fleet carries the
  peak with ingest down. This is [the click analytics buffer](../../reliability/queues-and-backpressure/#the-click-analytics-buffer).
- **Size**: 3,125 clicks a second per server × 300 s ≈ 940,000 clicks. At 64 bytes each, about 60 MB per server.
  Keep it on local disk if a crash must not lose clicks.
- **Breaker**: open when half the batches fail over 10 s. While open, the uploader stops calling and the buffer
  fills. After 30 s, go half open and send one probe batch, as in
  [probe gently](../../reliability/circuit-breakers/#probe-gently).
- **Full buffer**: drop the oldest clicks, count the drops, and mark that window on the dashboard as incomplete.
- **Drain**: cap uploads at twice the normal rate, so the backlog does not flatten ingest as it recovers.

</details>

## Step 4: An idempotency key

Make the Shorten button safe to press twice. Say what the client sends, what the server stores, and what a second
request gets while the first is still running.

<details>
<summary>Show one possible answer</summary>

- **Client**: makes a random key when the form opens and sends it as an Idempotency-Key header on every try.
- **Server**: inserts the key with status pending under a unique constraint, creates the link, then marks the key
  done with the code, all in one transaction on the primary. A duplicate that finds done gets the stored code. One
  that finds pending gets 409 with Retry-After: 1. A pending key also carries a timeout of about 30 seconds, so a
  server that crashes mid create cannot block the key forever. See
  [duplicates that arrive while the first is still running](../../reliability/idempotency/#duplicates-that-arrive-while-the-first-is-still-running).
- **Retention**: keep keys 24 hours. 100 creates a second × 86,400 ≈ 8.6 million rows a day, about 1 GB at 120
  bytes each.
- **Scope**: tie each key to the account, so two users never share a result.

</details>

## Step 5: Load shedding and 429

When ingest recovers at 8:09, readers, edges, and creators all come back at once. Bound each server's line, choose
what to reject first, and say what a rejected client hears.

<details>
<summary>Show one possible answer</summary>

- **Bounded line**: 64 requests per server. With 16 workers at 2.5 ms, a full line waits 64 × 2.5 ÷ 16 = 10 ms, well
  inside the deadline. A request that finds the line full gets an immediate 503 with Retry-After: 1. See
  [push back when the line is full](../../reliability/queues-and-backpressure/#push-back-when-the-line-is-full).
- **Shed order**: dashboard reads and the bulk API go first, creates next, and redirects last.
- **Create limit**: a token bucket per account allows 10 creates a second with bursts of 20. Past the limit, answer
  429 with Retry-After: 1, as in
  [telling clients to slow down](../../reliability/rate-limiting/#telling-clients-to-slow-down).
- **Clients**: wait Retry-After plus jitter, and stop after 3 tries.

</details>

## Step 6: Dashboards and burn rate

Pick the alert that would have paged at 8:04, and name the two views an on-call engineer opens first.

<details>
<summary>Show one possible answer</summary>

- **Budget**: a 99.9% SLO leaves 0.1% of redirects to fail, about 43 minutes of full outage in 30 days.
- **Burn rate**: at 8:04, about 99.8% of redirects failed, a burn rate near 1,000. At that pace the month's budget
  lasts about 43 minutes. Page when the burn rate passes 14.4 over both the last 5 minutes and the last hour, and open
  a ticket for slow burns.
- **Views**: RED on redirects shows rate down, errors up, and duration at the deadline. USE on workers shows 100%
  utilization and a full line. See
  [RED and USE](../../reliability/observability/#two-ways-to-read-a-dashboard-red-and-use).
- **Cause**: one trace shows the ingest span filling 3 s of a 3 s redirect.

</details>

## Trade-offs

<TradeoffTable title="Choices sho.rt made on a bad day">

| Choice                            | What it buys                            | What it costs                                  |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Click report into a local buffer  | Redirects ignore ingest health          | Counts lag, and a long outage drops clicks     |
| A timeout plus one deadline       | A hung call frees its worker            | A few slow but healthy calls fail              |
| One retry layer, 10% budget       | Load rises at most 10% in an outage     | Some failures a second retry would have saved  |
| Breaker on the uploader           | Ingest gets room to recover             | Counts pause while the breaker is open         |
| Idempotency keys                  | One link per press of Shorten           | A keys table and a daily cleanup               |
| Bounded lines, 503s, and 429s     | Fast, honest rejections                 | Some users see errors during a surge           |

</TradeoffTable>

<KeyTakeaways>

- A side job on the request path can take down the whole site: one 3 s call cut each server from 4,500 redirects a
  second to about 5.
- A timeout frees workers, and a 20 ms wait still caps the fleet near 11,000 a second. Remove the wait.
- Retries multiply across layers: 3 edge tries × 4 app tries = 12 calls per click. Retry at one layer, inside a 10%
  budget.
- A local buffer and a circuit breaker keep redirects fast while analytics is down. A capped drain protects it on the
  way back.
- An idempotency key with a pending state makes Shorten safe to press twice.
- Bounded lines and 429s with Retry-After shed load early, and burn rate alerts page within minutes.

</KeyTakeaways>
