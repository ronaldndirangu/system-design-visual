## The failure: analytics hangs, redirects go down

In [Queues and backpressure](../queues-and-backpressure/), a redirect wrote its click to a bounded buffer, and an
uploader shipped the buffer to analytics. This lesson starts from a **deliberately unsafe design** that drops that
queue so the failure is easy to see: each redirect calls the analytics ingest service and waits before it answers. The
end of the lesson puts the buffer back.

Each redirect server has 16 workers and a queue of 64. Every ingest call has a 500 ms timeout, as in
[Timeouts and cancellation](../timeouts/), and a failed call's click goes to a small local buffer. At 3 s, ingest
hangs: it accepts calls and never answers, until 12 s.

## Why a timeout was not enough

A timeout bounds how long one call waits, and every redirect still waits. On a normal day a redirect takes about
50 ms, so 16 workers finish about 320 a second against 150 arriving. During the hang each redirect holds a worker for
510 ms, so 16 workers finish about 31 a second. The queue is full by 3.6 s, and new redirects get a 503. A service the
redirect never needed took it down: a **cascading failure**.

A **circuit breaker** stops the calls while the dependency is broken. It is a small state machine around calls to one
dependency, named after the fuse box switch that cuts an overloaded circuit.

<Analogy>

A shop clerk phones the supplier to log every sale. One day the phone just rings. A sensible clerk notices that the
last few calls went unanswered, writes sales in a notebook, and tries one call every few minutes. When calls get
through again, the clerk phones in the notebook.

</Analogy>

Where it breaks: the clerk can hang up at will, while the breaker learns of a hung call only when its timeout fires.

## The three states

- **Closed.** Calls go through, and the breaker counts timeouts, refusals, and errors as failures. If at least half of
  20 or more calls in the last second failed, it **opens**.
- **Open.** Calls skip ingest: the redirect buffers the click in 10 ms and answers. After a 2 s **cooldown**, the
  breaker turns **half-open**.
- **Half-open.** 3 calls go through as **probes**. If all succeed, it **closes**. If any fails, it opens for another 2 s.

The **minimum call count** keeps the breaker calm on a normal day: 2% random errors is about 3 failures in 150 calls,
far below half, and one unlucky call can never trip it.

<details>
<summary>Go deeper: why the breaker took 1.1 s to open</summary>

Ingest hung at 3 s, and the breaker opened at 4.1 s. A hung call counts as a failure only when its 500 ms timeout
fires, and the 1 s window still held the healthy calls from just before 3 s. Once those aged out, 18 of the last 20
calls showed as failed, and the breaker tripped. Before then the queue filled, and 70 redirects got the breaker lane's
only 503s. A tighter timeout surfaces failures sooner, at the cost of cutting off slow calls that would have worked.

</details>

## Probe gently

Half-open is risky: the dependency may still be broken, or it may have just restarted with cold caches. A breaker with
no probe limit lets every redirect through until the round is decided.

## Put the buffer back

In the duel, the breaker lane buffered 1,561 of its 2,943 clicks. That fallback points at the safe design. Restore the
pipeline from [Queues and backpressure](../queues-and-backpressure/), and move the breaker to the uploader:

- A redirect writes its click to the bounded buffer and answers. It never calls ingest.
- An uploader drains the buffer to ingest, and the breaker wraps the uploader's calls.
- When ingest hangs, the breaker opens and the uploader stops calling. The buffer fills, then drops and counts clicks.
- When ingest heals, the probes pass, the breaker closes, and the uploader drains the backlog at a steady rate.

A hang now costs some clicks and never costs redirects.

Some dependencies have no fallback: without the links database, a redirect has no long URL. The breaker then fails
fast with a 503 and a Retry-After header, and callers that retry, as in [Retries with backoff and jitter](../retries/),
should stop while it is open. A **bulkhead** also caps how many workers may wait on one dependency, say 4 of 16.

<details>
<summary>Go deeper: alerts for an open breaker</summary>

An open breaker hides a broken dependency. Visitors still get their redirects, so a dashboard of redirect errors stays
green while analytics loses clicks. Alert on the breaker and the buffer too, and save pages for problems that need a
human now. Sort these five signals into **page** or **warn**:

1. Redirect errors burn the 30 day error budget 14 times faster than the SLO allows.
2. The ingest breaker has stayed open for 10 minutes.
3. The breaker flapped open for 3 s twice in the last hour.
4. The buffer dropped 0.1% of clicks in the last hour.
5. Consumer lag on the click log reaches 5 hours, and the log keeps 6 hours of data.

**Answer.** Page on 1 and 5. A burn rate of 14 spends a month's budget in about two days, as in
[SLOs and measurement](../../thinking/slos/). Lag near retention means the log will delete clicks before analytics
reads them. Warn on 2, 3, and 4. A breaker open for 10 minutes is doing its job while the buffer holds the clicks;
promote it to a page when the buffer nears full. Brief flaps and a trickle of drops deserve a ticket, and they become
pages only if they keep growing.
[Observability](../observability/) covers how to build these alerts.

</details>

## Trade-offs

<TradeoffTable title="Protecting a caller from a failing dependency">

| Choice                                   | What it buys                                                   | What it costs                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Timeouts only                            | A bounded wait per call, with no extra state                   | A hang still holds every worker: 1,016 redirects turned away      |
| Breaker with a fallback                  | The main job keeps working, and the dependency gets fewer calls | Needs somewhere to put the work, and code to replay it            |
| Breaker that fails fast with a 503       | Frees workers at once and gives callers a clear signal         | Requests that need the dependency still fail, just quickly        |
| Buffer in front, breaker on the uploader | Redirects never wait on analytics                              | A long outage fills the buffer and drops clicks                   |
| Low threshold or short window            | Opens sooner when the dependency breaks                        | Opens on noise without a minimum call count                       |
| Long cooldown                            | Less load on a struggling dependency                           | Slower to notice recovery                                         |
| No probe limit when half-open            | Reconnects a little sooner                                     | Every caller probes: a p95 of 460 ms against 60 ms                |

</TradeoffTable>

## Common mistakes

- Relying on timeouts alone: a hang still fills every worker.
- Opening on any error, with no minimum call count.
- Letting every caller probe when half-open.
- Calling a side service in the request path when a buffer could take the work.
- Retrying while the breaker is open, or never alerting when it stays open.

<KeyTakeaways>

- A timeout bounds how long each call waits. A breaker bounds how many calls wait on a broken dependency.
- Open on a failure threshold plus a minimum call count, cool down, then probe.
- Probe with a few calls: the cooldown sets when, and the probe limit sets how hard.
- Put side work behind a buffer with the breaker on the uploader, so an outage costs clicks and never redirects.
- An open breaker hides the outage from visitors, so page when it stays open.

</KeyTakeaways>

<Challenge>

Before redirecting, sho.rt's servers ask a URL safety service whether the long URL is known malware. It usually answers
in 20 ms, but twice this month it hung for ten minutes, and redirects failed across the site. A known bad link must
never redirect without a warning. Design the timeout, the breaker settings, and what a redirect does while the breaker

<div slot="answer">

- **A tight timeout**, about 100 ms, so a hung call holds a worker briefly and the breaker sees failures fast.
- **A breaker per server**: open when half of 20 or more recent calls fail, cool down for a few seconds, then close
  once a handful of probes succeed.
- **A bulkhead** that caps the workers waiting on the check, for the hang the breaker has not caught yet.
- **Cached verdicts with a short expiry.** While the breaker is open, redirect links with an unexpired clean verdict
  and show a warning page for the rest. The safety service pushes newly flagged links to every server, and the expiry
  bounds how long a stale clean verdict can slip through if that feed stalls too.
- **No retries while open, and an alert when it stays open**, since visitors will barely notice.

</div>

</Challenge>
